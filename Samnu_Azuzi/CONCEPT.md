**CONCEPT OF THE OPERA ŠAMNU AZUZI**

**Version:** 1.0

**Identity**

Title: *Šamnu Azuzi* (Akkadian: "Strong Oil" / "Mighty Fat" — a metaphor for the essence of life and death in the epic). Genre: opera in five acts, seventeen scenes. Total duration: approximately 195 minutes (3 hours 15 minutes) with one intermission (20 minutes after Act II or III). Main roles (6): Gilgamesh (baritone), Enkidu (tenor), Ishtar (soprano), Utnapishtim (bass), Shamhat (mezzo-soprano), Humbaba (bass). Chorus: 18–24 performers, mixed (SATB). Libretto and music: Jaba Tqemaladze. Primary host organization: Sulkalmakhi NGO (Abastumani, Georgia). Source: Standard Babylonian Version of the Epic of Gilgamesh (XII tablets).

**Artistic Concept**

The opera *Šamnu Azuzi* does not aim at an ethnographic reconstruction of ancient music or a geographical illustration. The central musical argument of the work is polyphony as a structural principle, expressing the multiplicity of forces and voices acting in the epic. The simultaneous sounding of several vocal lines is interpreted not as a stylistic device but as a dramaturgical necessity: gods, humans, demons, nature, and fate exist simultaneously; their voices overlap, creating conflict and harmony. Polyphony, therefore, is not a borrowing from folk tradition but a musical equivalent of the theological and existential polyphony of the epic.

**Dramaturgical Structure**

Five acts correspond to the key phases of the narrative: friendship and the campaign (Acts I–II), the death of Enkidu and the search for immortality (Act III), the encounter with Utnapishtim (Act IV), return and acceptance of mortality (Act V). Seventeen scenes unfold the narrative sequentially, without flashbacks. The thematic catalogue includes 20 motifs (T1–T20) that run through the entire score, creating a system of leitmotifs tied to characters, emotional states, and key concepts (friendship, death, water, immortality, wrath of the gods).

**Musical Language**

The opera uses a hybrid vocal technique. For the six main roles, 2–3 sections in each are performed in a folk vocal style (not throughout, but selectively — in climactic or ritual moments). The primary vocal style is Western operatic technique. The choral part is assigned to a folk choir (not an opera choir), creating a contrast between the "civilized" voice of the soloists and the "archaic" sound of the mass. Training for opera singers (8–10 hours) in Georgian polyphonic technique is planned before rehearsals begin. Instrumentation: symphony orchestra with the inclusion of select folk instruments (according to score v5).

**Status**

The score opus has reached version v5, which has undergone an internal review process (author + LLM-assisted critical review in three rounds + one consultation with a Georgian ethnomusicologist, name to be confirmed after obtaining consent). This is not an independent academic review. For academic publication, external blinded review through ICTM or Ethnomusicology Forum is required. The project is not considered ready for public release: a live workshop reading of the first scene of Act I (approximately 45 minutes) with real performers is needed — 3–4 opera singers and 6–8 performers from an amateur choir trained in folk technique.
