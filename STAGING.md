# Staging Notes — Threshold Stand & Samnu Azuzi

## Threshold Stand (28′41″)

A concept opera in three acts with overture. Composed through the Ze state machine.

### Orchestra
- Violins I (8 players)
- Violins II (6)
- Violas (5)
- Celli (4) + Double Basses (2)
- Flutes (2) + Oboe (1)
- French Horns (4)
- Trumpets (2) + Trombone (1)
- Timpani (1 player, 4 drums)
- Harp (1)

Total: 35 players.

### Voices
- Tenor (Act I, Act III)
- Soprano (Act II)
- Baritone (Act II)
- Mixed chorus, 18–24 voices, SATB

### Stage Directions

**Overture.** House lights half. Orchestra alone. The eight states cycle introduces all thematic material. No action on stage — the overture is a musical summary.

**Act I — The Mountain Calls.** Lights up on a mountain village. Backdrop: Caucasus peaks, stone towers. Villagers in traditional Georgian dress. The tenor (Hero) enters stage left, climbing. Chorus enters gradually from stage right, forming a semicircle. The Elder (spoken role, optional) delivers the prophecy from a raised platform. During the Conflict scene, red lighting. The act ends with the village forming a circle, facing outward — they have decided to stand.

**Act II — The Golden Balance.** The valley below. Warm golden light. A festival. The Duet (Tenor + Soprano) at center stage. The Dance-Groove brings the full chorus in celebration. The Baritone's aria of doubt is delivered from the edge of the stage, apart from the crowd. The Ritual scene: slow, processional, candles or lanterns. Act ends with the couple reunited at center.

**Act III — The Threshold Stand.** Storm lighting — strobes, blue-white flashes. The full chorus in battle formation. The Hero's aria ("I will stand") delivered from the highest point on stage. The Battle is choreographed — stylized, not literal. Victory: full golden light, chorus triumphant. Lament: single spotlight on the Hero, kneeling. Rebirth: dawn lighting, slow. Final Chorus: full company, facing the audience. The last note — silence — lights out.

### Technical Requirements
- Lighting: warm/cool wash, 4 specials, strobe capability
- Backdrop: mountain panorama (Act I), valley (Act II), storm sky (Act III)
- Props: candles/lanterns (Act II), ceremonial staff (Elder)
- Sound: no amplification needed for chamber orchestra

## Samnu Azuzi — The Gilgamesh Triad (14′32″)

Three concert arias from the opera Samnu Azuzi, based on the Standard Babylonian Version of the Epic of Gilgamesh (XII tablets).

### Orchestra
Same as Threshold Stand (35 players).

### Soloists
- Gilgamesh: dramatic baritone (range A2–F4)
- Enkidu: lyric tenor (range C3–A4)
- Ishtar: coloratura soprano (range C4–C6, with krimanchuli ornaments up to E6)

### Performance Notes

**Gilgamesh: "I Am King" (5′10″).** The king declares his divinity and his friendship with Enkidu. Baritone must balance heroic declamation with the vulnerability that foreshadows tragedy. The "Two-Thirds Divine" section (recitative) should be delivered with growing unease. The "Victory Over Humbaba" section calls for brass fanfares — the trumpets should stand.

**Enkidu: "The Wild One Dies" (4′22″).** The death aria. Tenor begins from offstage (or with back to audience), turning gradually. The "House of Dust" section is the emotional core — the vision of the underworld. Orchestra reduces to strings and harp. The final note should fade to nothing.

**Ishtar: "Wrath of the Goddess" (5′00″).** Fury and divine pride. Soprano enters at full power — no warmup. The krimanchuli ornaments in the "I Am Beyond Mortal" section require Georgian folk technique. The Bull of Heaven section: full orchestra, timpani thunder. The aria ends abruptly — the goddess does not resolve; she merely departs.

### Concert Staging
- Three music stands, three spots
- Arias performed in narrative order: Gilgamesh → Enkidu → Ishtar
- No intermission between arias — attacca transitions (10-second pauses)
- Translations projected above stage (English from Akkadian)
- Concert dress: formal black

### Rehearsal Schedule (minimum)
- Orchestra alone: 2 rehearsals (3 hours each)
- Soloists with piano: 2 sessions per aria
- Full run with orchestra: 1 rehearsal
- Dress rehearsal: 1

### Georgian Polyphonic Training
Soprano and tenor require 6–8 hours of Georgian folk vocal technique training before rehearsals. Focus on: parallel movement, open-throat production, krimanchuli ornamentation.
